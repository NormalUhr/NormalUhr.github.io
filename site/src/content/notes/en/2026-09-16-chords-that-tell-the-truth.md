The chords of a song can echo the melody, and they can also set off the lyrics and the theme of the song. I recently went through the chords of Yoga Lin's *Fairy Tale* (说谎, which literally means telling lies) and *Detour* (兜圈) closely, and found that both are excellent examples of what I just said. The lyrics of *Fairy Tale* have one very obvious feature. Every line insists that the singer is not lying, and every line is in fact a lie. Yoga Lin's distinctive voice brings out exactly that feeling of being stubborn, hurt and full of regret all at once.

If we look at the whole piece, we find that as far as the main melody goes, the entire song is in B flat major, with not a single note from outside the key. That is a very, very strange thing. You would think that when you sing a love song, some movement outside the key is unavoidable, such as borrowing from the minor while you are in the major. I have seen very few pieces that stay in one key from beginning to end, so for a song with this much story in it, calling the complete absence of out-of-key notes unexpected, or even calling it protesting too much, would not be going too far.

A major key usually carries feelings that are positive, optimistic and happy, so you would expect this song to express something positive as well. But do not forget that this song is called 说谎, telling lies. Here we can say that the lie in the melody is exposed by the chords. If you look at this song closely, then apart from the chorus it is almost entirely a dense weave of major chords with minor chords running through them, such as the Bb - Ebm (the minor four) - Bb - Ebm at the opening. There are so many minor chords here that listing them one by one is not even possible. The arranger was already trying to say something at this point. Under the show of strength on the surface, which is the melody, what is hidden is resentment, regret and pain. And most importantly, right through to the end of the song, although the melody brings us back to the tonic of the major key, in the chords you never get a real resolution. The writer ends the piece on the minor four, which is strongly symmetrical with the introduction, and the intention behind that has already been stated plainly in the lyrics: "祝你做个幸福的新娘，我的心事情你全遗忘", may you be a happy bride, and forget everything that was on my mind. The voice, like the lyrics, finishes frankly and cleanly, but what the chords express is the feeling that was never said out loud. You are getting married, I wish you happiness, but please do not forget me.

So the sadness of a song does not have to be shown through movement outside the key in the melody. It can instead be told to the listener quietly through the changes in the chords, which also fits the idea of the song itself to a certain degree. Standing opposite that is *Detour*. This sweet summing up of the singer's own love can be captured by the saying "if you want it sweet, put the salt in first". The four bars of the introduction already contain the whole tonal movement of the piece: major (Dmaj7-F#m7-Em7), then minor (Am7/C, which is the five of the minor), then major (Gmaj7-A7), like the ups and downs of love going around in a circle.

This piece is in D major. Unlike *Fairy Tale*, the first half of the verse here is a very clean and bright major chord progression. Only at the pre-chorus ("你打呵欠，我游花园，差一点多一些") does a real switch between major and minor arrive. The pre-chorus is a fairly clean pairing of major against minor. The major part is G - F#m7 ("你打呵欠"), then comes the minor Gm7 - Am7 ("*我*游花*园*") - Bbmaj7 ("差一*点*"), and then back to the major A7 ("多一些"). The italics above mark the sixth and seventh degrees, lowered because D major has turned into D minor. So taken as a whole it runs like this: the four of the major, the three of the major, the four of the minor, the five of the minor, the six of the minor, the six of the major. You will find that this circling back and forth between major and minor is a true picture of love. No relationship goes entirely smoothly, but a love that is good will always return to clear skies, which is to say it returns to the major. Once the chorus proper begins, minor notes and minor chords appear in the second phrase as well. On the words 是 and 而 in "一种浪漫的爱是浪费时间" and "错过了太久反而错得完美无缺", the C# reverts to C natural, which is the seventh degree dropping by a semitone as the major turns into the minor. The chord that answers it is the Am7 from the minor. Most importantly, once the chorus ends, the whole piece returns to a standard authentic cadence and gives you an ending you can enjoy. The chords of this piece are just like its lyrics. They wheel back and forth between major and minor, going around in circles, and in the end they come back to where they started, under clear skies.

So these two pieces show, in a very plain way, how chords can work together with the mood of the lyrics. I hope I am not reading too much into them, because I think both of these pieces really are beautiful.
