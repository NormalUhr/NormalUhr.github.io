> Knowledge is a curse.

No one can use AI to write articles or make videos that explain to others something they do not understand themselves. Sadly, countless so-called AI popular-science creators have not understood this. AI has greatly lowered the barrier to making popular-science content. It lets people easily produce flashy articles and videos that seem to make a lot of sense, to introduce ideas and explain how things work. But I have seen far too much content like this. Every sentence seems reasonable on its own, yet put together they do not follow even the most basic logic. At first, I really did approach it wanting to learn, and I often put my failure to understand down to my own lack of ability or background knowledge. It was only when I saw someone explaining a field in which I could myself be called an expert that I realised how low the quality of this content was. AI is a textbook case of the “curse of knowledge”. It knows so much that it has forgotten how to talk to ordinary people whose knowledge is limited, and teaching suffers the most. It has another problem as well. It often assumes that the user knows terms, concepts, and definitions that it made up itself, so the content looks convincing, yet even people in the field cannot understand it right away. More than once, I have found that AI actually raises the cost of communication. This cost arises directly between people and AI, and as more and more people choose AI as their proxy for communicating, it is also raising the cost of communication between people. Every sentence seems free of errors, yet put together the logic and the meaning do not hold together, there is no central argument to be found, and no clear meaning can be read from it. In spreading and sharing knowledge, this problem is especially fatal. So AI can bring knowledge, but it cannot replace the process of learning. Those who spread knowledge should all the more guard against arrogance and impatience. Otherwise, they are merely adding more noise to an online world whose signal-to-noise ratio is already extremely low.
