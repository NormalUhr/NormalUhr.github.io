I am not someone who dislikes solitude. To some extent, I enjoy solitude. Solitude gives me the chance to examine myself with care.

To go from accepting solitude to enjoying it is, in a sense, to become self-consistent when you are alone. Facing someone close to you takes courage, and so does facing yourself. In *The Big Bang Theory*, Leonard’s mother, a psychologist, once pointed out that even after they married, Leonard and Penny would subconsciously avoid being alone together. As a result, they tended to pull other people into their lives, Sheldon for example, to forcibly scatter the attention that might start to seem excessive the moment they let their guard down, and so avoid awkwardness. Isn’t our relationship with ourselves much the same? We crave other people’s attention, yet we fear our own gaze.

“Everyone is a slave to something. I suppose none of us can go on living without finding something to be drunk on.” Finding something to be drunk on seems to be the most legitimate and the simplest way of running away from ourselves. Like gears set into the vast machine of society, we keep turning, day after day. The past, the present, and the future concern us at every point, and yet they seem to have nothing to do with us. Only in moments of solitude can I look back on the road I have walked. The resilient me, the contemptible me, the selfish me, the clever me. I have gone from blaming myself and flagellating myself to affirming myself, and then to making peace with myself.
