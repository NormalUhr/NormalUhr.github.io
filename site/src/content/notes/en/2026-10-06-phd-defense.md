Today I finished my PhD defense and became a real Doctor. The slides I made for the faculty job market last year needed only a small edit before they turned into a decent set of defense slides. The PhD years left me with plenty of memories that make me laugh and wince at the same time. The most absurd of them is a group meeting. In March 2024, around spring break, I slipped off to Orlando for a week and somehow my advisor never found out. I went to three Disney parks on that trip, and literally one second I was riding Soarin' Around the World at Epcot and the next I was sitting on the floor of the standby queue hall with my laptop open, running a group meeting. Another time I sat in on a project meeting while watching the fireworks, and luckily I did not have to say anything. The moment I arrived at Animal Kingdom I bought a Disney Mickey ears headband, the graduation cap kind, and two years later I really did graduate wearing it. I am grateful that I ended up with a good advisor. There was none of what people usually mean by the ordeal of a PhD. It was much more a matter of chasing, one after another, the goals I had set for myself. I think Professor Liu put it very well. A PhD student should develop themselves the way you would develop a performer. A performer needs a body of work, needs exposure, needs hype, needs an audience, and above all needs real ability and strong professional skill. I do not like the phrase "a new beginning". It is like training an LLM, where a new beginning usually means the previous run collapsed. I would rather carry the momentum I already have into a brand new loss landscape and explore there.
