Vectors are not an easy concept for beginners to accept. For those of us who have known line segments since primary school, it seems hard to truly feel that vectors exist anywhere outside maths and physics problems.

After graduating from university, I moved from place to place, but my life always came down to a fixed back and forth between two places. Perhaps it was home and school, perhaps home and the office, or perhaps home and the sports hall. Every time I moved somewhere new, the way there and the way back would both become very familiar, and yet it often took a long time before it suddenly struck me that I had been walking the same road back and forth all along. This surprise, at once familiar and strange, still catches me off guard from time to time. That is because, in my mind, the two had always looked different from each other, as if they were two different places.

It makes sense when I think about it. The way there and the way back come at different times, with different moods, different weather, different things to see, and different things on my mind, so naturally the memories they leave behind are different too. And at the very moment I realised this, the concept of a vector suddenly became concrete. Two opposite directions along the same road seemed to cross time and meet in my mind.
