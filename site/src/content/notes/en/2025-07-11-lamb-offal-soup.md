The hardest part of losing weight is the evening, when I really want a bowl of lamb offal soup. The best lamb offal soup I have had in my life was still the one back in middle school. I got up a little after five, because I had to bike to class by six thirty for morning reading. In winter the cold wind blew until your hands hurt, and halfway there Song Xuan and I would park our bikes at a breakfast stall, sit at a low little table under one dim lamp, and order a bowl of steaming lamb offal soup with a spoonful of chili, which had to be the chili made from rendered lamb fat that had already set into a solid block, and with it a bowl of crisp shredded pancake or some pan fried buns. After that I could stay sharp all morning, and I never knew what a carb crash even was. Middle school was when I worked the hardest, and after that, unless I was rushing a deadline, I never again got up a little after five to read or study or work. Maybe it is because you get out what you put in, and I have never had lamb soup that good since either.
