Humans are, by nature, power-loving animals.

So do not start a company with good friends, do not take on a project with good friends, and do not even write a paper with good friends. When all is calm, do not try to “share” future power with anyone. With good friends, having fun together is enough.

Seen from this angle, marriage really is the tomb of love. Marriage means a redistribution of control over one’s own life, and this redistribution of power runs through every part of life. Two people in love may not want to control each other, yet each may be unwilling to be restricted by the other. This tension does not wait for love to fade before it shows up. Two people can still love each other and still cherish the years they have spent together, yet argue endlessly over things that seem trivial. As the argument goes on, how the matter is actually arranged stops being so important. What is hard to let go of is why you cannot decide even this for yourself.

So being willing to share your life while in love does not necessarily mean being willing to share power afterwards. When power is briefly handed over during the honeymoon phase, the lender sees it as an investment, while the recipient treats it as a trust. Many of the failed marriages I have seen trace back to this.
