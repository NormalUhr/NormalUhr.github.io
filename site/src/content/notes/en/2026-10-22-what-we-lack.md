The more a person lacks something, the more they value it. The more they value it, the more they tend to show it off. And the more they show it off, the more readily they use it to find fault with others. Bound by their own experience, people always assign too high a prior probability to how much the things they have never had would add to their happiness. I am no exception. I always dwell on the things I never managed to get but care about deeply, and I subconsciously believe they are crucial to the “success” I have not yet achieved. And at certain critical moments, this bias always comes back to devour me. The things I already have, by contrast, I tend to take for granted, and I underestimate the good they have done me on the road that brought me here.

Rural parents who sell vegetables believe that knowledge changes destiny, while a couple with PhDs who are starting their own business may feel that doing a PhD is nothing more than a game about papers, and that capital is what really matters. And what do the rich long for? Could it be the way the couple selling vegetables stand by each other through hard times? I have no way of knowing.

But I do know this. As the saying goes, “What we cannot have will always keep us restless.” It is the things we have never had that build our own fortress besieged, where those outside want to get in and those inside want to get out. It is easy for a person to see how the things they do not yet have hold them back, but hard to judge how much what they already have has helped them. This is true of career, resources, love, and talent alike.
